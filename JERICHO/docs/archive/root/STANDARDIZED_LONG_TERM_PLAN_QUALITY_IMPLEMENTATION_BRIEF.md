# STANDARDIZED_LONG_TERM_PLAN_QUALITY_IMPLEMENTATION_BRIEF

## 1. Purpose

Stage 3 exists to formalize what counts as a good long-horizon plan before
initial feasibility and live P.O.S.

Stage 3 extends Stage 2 from local plan structure quality into temporal
structure quality across weeks and months.

The immediate practical need is to prevent long-term plans from collapsing into
vague abstraction, false precision, unreadable density, or unrealistic pacing.

---

## 2. Canonical Role

Standardized Long-Term Plan Quality is the governing standard for evaluating
canonical multi-phase and longer-horizon plans across the canonical 45.

This standard must drive:

- long-horizon plan generation expectations
- chart inspectability over time
- pacing realism
- checkpoint structure
- uncertainty treatment
- later feasibility inputs

Long-term plan quality is about temporal structural truth, not just more blocks.

---

## 3. Long-Term Plan Quality Pillars

### Phase coherence

The plan shows meaningful temporal shifts in work mode, readiness, or output
instead of one undifferentiated horizon.

### Pacing realism

Work is distributed across the horizon in a way that is temporally believable
under the stated constraints and dependencies.

### Temporal inspectability

A reviewer can inspect how work evolves over time rather than only seeing local
rows or aggregate totals.

### Uncertainty honesty

The plan preserves honest provisionality where distant structure cannot yet be
represented with full certainty.

### Checkpoint discipline

The plan includes reevaluation points where the horizon or plan integrity should
realistically be revisited.

### Prep/execution balance over time

The plan shows a believable temporal relationship between readiness work and
substantive output work.

### Saturation control

The plan avoids sustained overload or unreadable temporal density across the
horizon.

### Continuity across horizon

The plan remains structurally connected from early setup through later execution
and review without temporal fragmentation.

---

## 4. Phase Decomposition Standard

Long-horizon plans should be decomposed into meaningful phases where
appropriate.

Phases must represent real shifts in work mode, readiness state, or output
pattern, not arbitrary date slicing.

Phases must be distinguishable from one another.

Phases should support visible lineage into deliverables, actions, and blocks.

A long-term plan should not appear as one undifferentiated block field if phase
logic is materially present.

Phase decomposition must not create fake complexity when the plan is genuinely
simple.

---

## 5. Long-Horizon Pacing Standard

Work should be distributed in a way that is temporally believable.

Long-term plans must avoid front-loaded compression without justification.

Long-term plans must avoid back-loaded wishful packing.

Pacing should reflect dependency structure, preparation burden, and horizon
length.

Pacing should be inspectable, not hidden behind aggregate counts.

Temporal density should remain interpretable on chart and calendar surfaces.

---

## 6. Uncertainty Handling Standard

Uncertainty should be surfaced where the horizon genuinely reduces precision.

Distant work should not be represented with false exactness when only
provisional structure is honest.

Uncertainty should degrade trust where appropriate without collapsing the whole
plan.

Assumptions and uncertainty markers should remain distinct where possible.

Long-horizon plans should not silently convert speculative future structure into
full certainty.

---

## 7. Checkpoint and Review Standard

Long-term plans should include meaningful review and checkpoint structure where
appropriate.

Checkpoints must correspond to actual reevaluation points, not decorative
milestones.

Checkpoint cadence should reflect horizon length and plan volatility.

Checkpoints should help preserve plan integrity as execution reality changes.

Long-horizon plans should not assume static certainty across the entire horizon.

---

## 8. Prep vs Execution Balance Over Time

Long-horizon plans should not remain trapped in preparation too long without
visible execution emergence.

Long-horizon plans should not over-index execution before readiness work is
plausibly complete.

Prep and execution balance should be inspectable by phase or time segment where
possible.

Long-range plans should show believable transition from setup into substantive
output where the goal demands it.

Some lanes may legitimately remain prep-heavy longer, but that should be
structurally explainable.

---

## 9. Saturation Control Standard

Plans should avoid sustained overload without justification.

Plans should avoid visually or structurally unreadable density.

Saturation should be judged across time, not only per single day.

Repeated overload bands should degrade long-term plan quality.

Sparse horizons should also be inspectable so under-structured plans do not
masquerade as realism.

---

## 10. Long-Range Chart Inspectability Standard

Long-range plans must remain inspectable as temporal structure, not just local
row truth.

Phase boundaries or phase logic should be inspectable where canonically present.

Pacing and checkpoint structure should be visible enough to review.

Prep and execution distribution over time should be inspectable where data
supports it.

Long-horizon chart rendering must not collapse meaningful structure into
unreadable clutter or empty abstraction.

---

## 11. Long-Term Plan Quality States

### Trusted

Temporal structure is coherent, pacing is believable, uncertainty is handled
honestly, and the horizon remains inspectable.

### Provisional

The long-horizon plan is usable, but temporal certainty or pacing quality
remains partially incomplete or lightly degraded.

### Degraded

The long-horizon plan is materially weak in pacing, checkpoint discipline,
prep/execution balance, saturation control, or temporal inspectability.

### Withheld

The long-horizon plan is too temporally incoherent, structurally opaque, or
falsely precise to be treated as trustworthy planning truth.

These states are temporal structural states, not execution-performance states.

They must allow:

- a structurally solid but uncertainty-marked long-horizon plan to remain usable
- a dense but unreadable or implausibly paced long-horizon plan to degrade
- a temporally incoherent long-horizon plan to withhold when necessary

---

## 12. Acceptance Criteria

Long-term quality pillars are explicitly defined.

Phase, pacing, uncertainty, checkpoint, saturation, and inspectability standards
are explicit.

Prep and execution balance over time is explicitly addressed.

The standard is usable across the canonical 45 without becoming vague.

The standard can guide later implementation work for long-horizon plan
generation, chart surfaces, and feasibility inputs.

---

## 13. Failure Cases To Prevent

- one undifferentiated multi-month plan with no meaningful phase structure
- arbitrary phase labels with no real work-mode change
- unrealistic front-loaded workload packing
- unrealistic back-loaded workload packing
- false precision deep in the horizon
- no review or checkpoint structure in volatile long goals
- prep-heavy plans that never visibly transition toward execution
- unreadable long-range chart density
- sparse but structurally empty horizons being mistaken for realism
- standards so abstract they cannot guide implementation

---

## 14. Minimal Implementation Order

- define long-term quality pillars
- define phase decomposition and pacing standards
- define uncertainty and checkpoint standards
- define prep vs execution balance and saturation control standards
- define long-range inspectability standard
- define long-term quality states
- use the standard to drive later implementation passes on long-horizon
  generation and chart interpretation
- then use the standard to support later initial feasibility inputs
