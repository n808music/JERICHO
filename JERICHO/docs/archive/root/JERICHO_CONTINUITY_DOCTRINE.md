# JERICHO CONTINUITY DOCTRINE

## The Problem JERICHO Solves

Most planning systems treat plan changes as goal changes:

```
Plan changes → goal failed → system panic
```

This is wrong for long-horizon missions.

A better model:

```
Stable objective + Adaptive route + Entropy management = Continuity
```

## The Doctrine

### Principle 1: Core Goal Stability

The core objective should be **rare to change** and **hard to change** when it does.

Examples of core goals:
- "Build a major company"
- "Create art that matters"
- "Establish a movement"

**Not** core goals (these are campaigns):
- "Launch album by Q3"
- "Build SaaS by 2027"
- "Get on podcast top 100"

**Test**: If your goal changed every quarter, it wasn't your real goal.

### Principle 2: Route Adaptation

The route to the goal changes **constantly and easily**.

Examples of route changes:
- Pause album, accelerate app
- Move company to new city
- Take a job for income
- Switch technology stack
- Find new collaborators
- Change pricing model
- Adjust launch timing

**Why**: Entropy. The world changes. New information arrives. Constraints shift.

**Consequence**: Route changes are not goal changes.

### Principle 3: Problem Scale

Distinguish the scope of every problem:

| Scale | Example | Response | Mission Impact |
|-------|---------|----------|----------------|
| Block | One week delayed | Reschedule block | None |
| Cycle | Quarterly goal missed | Adjust next cycle | Local |
| Campaign | Market fit not found | Kill/pivot campaign | Strategic |
| Thesis | Fundamental contradiction | Review thesis | Existential |
| Objective | Mission becomes irrelevant | Reset mission | Existential |

**JERICHO's job**: Classify every disruption at the right scale, then act accordingly.

### Principle 4: The Tradeoff Matrix

Not all adjustments are equal.

**Allowed tradeoffs** (can be made freely):
- Timing (slip by weeks/months)
- Scope (do less now)
- Route (change campaigns)
- Sequence (reorder priorities)
- Tactics (change how we execute)

**Forbidden tradeoffs** (require mission reset):
- Ownership (can't give up company)
- Creative control (can't delegate direction)
- Non-negotiables (can't violate core values)
- Long-term vision (can't sacrifice future for present)

**Conditional tradeoffs** (allowed short-term with recovery plan):
- Income security (can take job if temporary)
- Campaign pause (can pause if resumable)
- Scope reduction (can reduce if expanded later)

### Principle 5: Entropy is Expected

Entropy is not failure. It's the environment.

**Entropy categories**:
```
Economic entropy:    Job loss, money pressure, unexpected cost, capital scarcity
Relational entropy:  Partner failure, team departure, co-founder conflict
Technical entropy:   Tool failure, platform change, architecture debt
Temporal entropy:    Missed window, sequencing error, unexpected delay
Environmental entropy: Moving cities, COVID, market shift, regulation
Personal entropy:    Fatigue, loss of confidence, health issues
```

**Doctrine**: Entropy is expected. The goal is not to eliminate it. The goal is to **preserve continuity through it**.

### Principle 6: Continuity is Victory

The real metric is not "did every task complete?"

The real metric is: **"Despite entropy, did the user move toward the durable objective?"**

Examples:

| Scenario | Task Completion | Continuity | Assessment |
|----------|-----------------|-----------|------------|
| Plan perfect, task missed | 95% | Continuous | Victory |
| Plan changed, route adapted | 60% | Continuous | Victory |
| Plan slipped 6 months | 40% | Continuous | Victory |
| Plan failed, mission abandoned | 30% | Broken | Defeat |

**Why**: A delayed task is local. A broken mission is existential.

### Principle 7: Transparency Over Opacity

Every major change should be recorded:
- **What** changed (objective, thesis, campaign, cycle, block)
- **Why** it changed (entropy event, market learning, capability discovery)
- **What stayed constant** (which non-negotiables held)
- **Whether continuity survived** (yes/no)

This creates a **mission narrative**: not just a plan, but a story of adaptation and continuity.

## The Hierarchy: What Changes and What Doesn't

```
STABLE ←────────────────────→ ADAPTIVE

Core Objective
    (rarely changes)
         ↓
Strategic Thesis
    (evolves on evidence)
         ↓
Active Campaigns
    (added/paused/merged/killed)
         ↓
Execution Cycles
    (rewritten constantly)
         ↓
Blocks / Tasks
    (move/reschedule often)
```

### Immutability Expectations

| Layer | Change Frequency | Requires | Reason |
|-------|------------------|----------|--------|
| Objective | <1x per 3 years | Existential doubt | Rarity makes it stable |
| Thesis | 1-2x per 5 years | Major market shift | Stability guides campaigns |
| Campaigns | Monthly | Market evidence | Adapt route constantly |
| Cycles | Every cycle | Performance data | Time-bounded attacks |
| Blocks | Every week | Execution reality | Disposable units |

## The Decision Framework

When disruption hits, ask:

**Q1: What broke?**
- One task? → Block issue
- One cycle? → Cycle issue
- One campaign? → Campaign issue
- The entire thesis? → Thesis issue
- The entire objective? → Mission reset

**Q2: Is this an allowed tradeoff?**
- Check against allowed list
- If yes: Make the change, record entropy event
- If no: Go to Q3

**Q3: Does this violate a non-negotiable?**
- Check against forbidden list
- If yes: Requires explicit mission decision
- If no: Make the change with caution

**Q4: Will continuity survive?**
- If yes: Proceed with adaptation
- If no: This is a mission-breaking event; decide whether to reset mission

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Confusing Campaigns with the Objective

**Wrong**: "My goal is to launch an album by Q3."

**Right**: "My goal is to build a company. The album is one campaign toward that goal."

**Why**: If the album fails, the company mission should survive.

### Mistake 2: Treating Every Delay as Mission Failure

**Wrong**: "We missed the Q3 launch. The mission is dead."

**Right**: "We missed Q3. Let's evaluate whether to adjust the timeline or pivot the campaign."

**Why**: Delays are local. They don't kill the mission.

### Mistake 3: Letting Income Security Become the Mission

**Wrong**: "I need to get a job. So my new goal is employment."

**Right**: "I need a job as temporary support for the company mission. When the company has runway, the job ends."

**Why**: Jobs are support, not mission. They should be temporary.

### Mistake 4: Never Saying "The Mission Changed"

**Wrong**: Always defending the original objective even when evidence says it won't work.

**Right**: "After 2 years, the original thesis failed. We're updating to a new mission."

**Why**: Missions should be durable but not dogmatic. Update if evidence requires it.

### Mistake 5: Abandoning Without Recording

**Wrong**: Quietly quitting, forgetting why you tried, starting a new mission without clarity.

**Right**: Explicit decision. Recorded. Rationale preserved.

**Why**: Next time you encounter entropy, you need to remember what you learned.

## The Strategic Questions

Ask these quarterly:

### On Continuity
- [ ] Am I still moving toward the core objective?
- [ ] What changed about the route?
- [ ] What stayed constant?
- [ ] Did any non-negotiables break?

### On Campaigns
- [ ] Which campaigns are active? Which are paused?
- [ ] Is the portfolio aligned with the thesis?
- [ ] Should any campaign be killed or accelerated?
- [ ] Are too many campaigns active (focus diffusion)?

### On Entropy
- [ ] What entropy events happened this quarter?
- [ ] Did I preserve continuity through them?
- [ ] What new constraints appeared?
- [ ] How should the route change?

### On Signals
- [ ] Are continuity signals green?
- [ ] Have derailment signals appeared?
- [ ] Is my confidence in the thesis still valid?
- [ ] Should I update the strategic thesis?

### On Commitment
- [ ] Do I still want to pursue this objective?
- [ ] Am I willing to adapt the route?
- [ ] Are my non-negotiables still true?
- [ ] Should I reset the mission?

## The Continuity Status Meanings

### 🟢 Aligned

**Meaning**: On course toward the durable objective.

**Signals**:
- Continuity signals are green
- Derailment signals are none/low
- Recent entropy has been corrected
- Plan is tracking to thesis

**Action**: Continue execution. Review annually.

**Message to self**: *"The mission is healthy. Stay the course."*

### 🟡 Strained

**Meaning**: Facing entropy but still pursuing mission.

**Signals**:
- Recent disruptions but corrected
- Some continuity signals yellow
- One or two medium derailment signals
- Campaigns are adapting

**Action**: Monitor closely. Increase review frequency. Prepare contingency plans.

**Message to self**: *"The mission survived this chaos. Be ready for more. Update the route if needed."*

### 🟠 Drifting

**Meaning**: Significant deviation from strategic thesis.

**Signals**:
- Multiple campaigns stalled or off-track
- High derailment signals active
- Continuity signals degrading
- Risk of entropy accumulation

**Action**: Strategic review required. Consider thesis refinement or campaign restructuring.

**Message to self**: *"The mission is at risk. This requires your full attention. Decide: refine the route or reset the mission."*

### 🔴 Endangered

**Meaning**: Risk of mission abandonment.

**Signals**:
- Critical derailment signals (ownership loss, core contradiction, financial collapse)
- Non-negotiable being breached
- Continuity signals failing
- Allowed tradeoffs exhausted

**Action**: Immediate mission reset or explicit decision to change mission.

**Message to self**: *"The mission is in danger. You must decide now: Can this mission survive? Should it?"*

## The Doctrine Summary

```
One goal. Many routes.
One objective. Multiple campaigns.
One mission. Countless adjustments.

Entropy is expected. Adaptation is victory.
Route changes are not goal changes.
Delay is not defeat.

Protect the core.
Question the thesis.
Adjust the campaign.
Rewrite the cycle.
Move the block.

That is continuity.
```

---

This doctrine turns planning from a single-point forecast into a continuous system for adapting the route while defending the mission.
